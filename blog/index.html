Where should a new candidate start?

Start with the board, because everything else follows from it. Commercial work over $10,000 means the Contractor's Licensing Board. Home building over $5,000 means the Residential Builders Commission. Then read South Carolina Contractor License: 15 Questions Answered for the 15 questions most people ask, and the licensing guide for the step-by-step process. When you know your classification, its license page lists the exams, the job limits and every book and course we carry for it.

  1. Decide between CLB and RBC by the type and value of your work.
  2. Pick the classification, such as Roofing or Residential Builder.
  3. Check the PSI bulletin for the book list and editions.
  4. Get the books 4 to 8 weeks before your exam date.
  5. Tab, practice and take 2 timed exams before test day.

How long should you study for a South Carolina exam?

Most candidates need 4 to 8 weeks. A simple plan that works for both CLB and RBC exams:

WeekFocus
1 to 2Learn the layout of each reference book; place tabs.
3 to 5Practice questions by topic; highlight the answers you look up.
6Business and Law practice: about 2.5 minutes per question.
7 to 82 or more full timed practice exams, then review misses.

Candidates with less time often choose highlighted and tabbed book sets or a live virtual class to shorten weeks 1 and 2.

What changed in South Carolina licensing recently?

  • May 2022: the residential specialty registration threshold rose from $200 to $500 per job.
  • May 19, 2023: the CLB commercial threshold rose from $5,000 to $10,000 under Act 69.
  • January 1, 2025: the Nonstructural Renovation classification began requiring a PSI technical exam.
  • October 29, 2026: the renewal deadline for 2026 to 2028 burglar and fire alarm contractor licenses.

Changes like these are why every post carries an updated date. Before you bid near a limit or book an exam, check the date on the post and the linked LLR page.

Which licenses do readers ask about most?

Five licenses account for most of the questions we get from South Carolina contractors. Each link goes to the license page with its exams, limits and study packages.

  • Residential Builder: A Residential Builder license from the Residential Builders Commission covers building and remodeling homes in South Carolina.
  • NASCLA Commercial General Building: The NASCLA Commercial General Building exam is the path to an Unlimited Building (UB) license, which lets a South Carolina general contractor build commercial structures of any height.
  • Roofing: The Roofing classification covers commercial roof systems, including low-slope membranes, metal panels, steep-slope roofing and flashing.
  • Commercial Business and Law: The Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors exam is required for every CLB general and mechanical license, and for alarm and pool licenses.
  • Residential HVAC: A Residential HVAC license from the Residential Builders Commission covers heating, air conditioning and ventilation work on homes.
